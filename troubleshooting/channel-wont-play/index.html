Troubleshooting

A channel that will not play

The useful question is not why this channel fails, but how it fails compared with the others. One dead channel, all channels dead, and a channel that plays with no picture are three separate problems.

Last updated 7 September 2026

Before changing any setting, try two or three other channels. Whether the failure is confined to one channel or affects all of them is the single most useful piece of information, and it takes ten seconds to establish.

What happensWhere to look
One channel fails, others are fineThat stream, or its decoder
Every channel failsYour account, your connection, or the server
Sound plays, picture is blackVideo decoding — the stream is arriving
Picture plays, no soundAudio decoding, or a track the device cannot handle
The channel is not in the list at allA group switched off, or the provider dropped it
It starts, then stops after a few secondsA second connection being refused

When nothing plays

An app that has been working and now plays nothing is usually a change on the account side, not on the device.

  • Connection limit. Close InoxTV completely on every other device — fully, not to the background — then try again. This is the most common cause and the easiest to miss, because the failure appears on the device you are sitting in front of rather than the one causing it.
  • Expired subscription. On an Xtream playlist, Settings › Playlists › Account Information shows the expiry date and the connection allowance. That screen is the fastest way to stop guessing.
  • No network. The app says so directly: No internet connection. Please check your network and try again. If that message appears, nothing else on this page applies.
  • Server down. Nothing works for anybody, and it comes back on its own. Resist the urge to change settings that were right an hour ago.

Backgrounding is not closing

On both phones and televisions, pressing Home leaves the app running and, on some providers, holding the connection. Close it from the recent‑apps view before you conclude the account is at fault.

When one channel fails

A single failing channel among hundreds that work is either a dead stream or a decoding mismatch, and the two are easy to tell apart: a decoding problem usually gives you something — audio, a black screen, a frozen frame — while a dead stream gives you nothing at all.

What you getWhat to try
Nothing, then Unable to play this content right nowProbably a dead stream. Try it again in an hour, then report it to your provider with the channel name.
Audio, black screenVideo decoder → Software.
Picture, no audioAudio track in the tool strip first, in case the channel offers a second one, then Audio decoder → Software.
Green blocks, tearing, or a frozen frame with running audioVideo decoder → Software. If it clears up, the hardware decoder does not handle that stream's format.
Plays for a second, then dropsA connection limit, or a server rejecting a second session.
Plays in another app but not hereTry both software decoders. If the other app carries a codec this one does not, that is the difference.

The decoder settings live under Settings › Playback, and the app's own note about them is worth taking at face value: try the software decoder if the device has trouble decoding with the hardware one. Hardware stays in the list behind your choice, so this cannot leave you worse off than you were. Software decoding does use more processing and more battery, and both rows apply to everything you play, so switch back once the channel you needed it for is done. The playback page covers the rest of that screen.

When the channel is not in the list at all

A channel that has vanished rather than failed is a different problem, and in this build there are only two explanations.

Its group is switched off. Settings › Playlists › Manage groups holds a visibility switch per group, and a group switched off disappears from the channel lists and the guide together. If you spent an evening tidying a large playlist, this is almost certainly where the channel went. Show all groups brings everything back at once, which is the quickest way to confirm it.

Your provider dropped it. Then it is not in your playlist any more, and no setting will produce it. Reloading the playlist confirms which of the two you are looking at: if it comes back, it was a group; if it does not, it is gone.

There is no per‑channel hiding or blocking in this build. The Block channel, Hide channel and Manage… rows you will find in the channel menu are laid out but not connected, so a channel cannot have been hidden individually even by accident. Parental controls gate areas of the settings rather than channels, so a PIN is never why a channel is missing.

Ruling out your own network

The test is the same one that settles buffering: play the channel on a phone with Wi‑Fi off, over mobile data.

  • Works on mobile data, fails at home. Something on your network is in the way. A different DNS server is the first thing to try, since some networks fail to resolve provider hostnames.
  • Fails on both. The stream or the account, not your network. Nothing in the app will change that.
  • Works at home, fails on mobile data. Either the mobile connection is too slow for that stream, or your provider is refusing a second connection while the television holds the first.

One thing that will not help

Every route to a second player app is closed in this build. Use external player under Settings › Playback, the Open in external player row in the channel menu and the button of the same name on a catalogue item are all locked, and each reports Available for premium users rather than launching anything.

It is worth stating plainly because it is a common suggestion and it will cost you time. If a stream will not play here, the decoder settings above are the whole toolkit — there is no escape hatch to a different app.

The order that saves the most time

  1. Try other channels

    One channel or all of them. Everything below depends on this answer.

  2. Close the app on other devices

    Free, quick, and the cause more often than anything else.

  3. Check the account

    Account Information if you are on Xtream Codes; your provider's page otherwise.

  4. Try the software decoders

    Only if you were getting partial playback. They will not revive a dead stream.

  5. Test on mobile data

    This separates your network from everything else, and it is the last thing you can determine without your provider.

If a channel plays but plays badly rather than failing, buffering and stuttering is the page you want. If nothing loaded in the first place, start at a playlist that will not load.

Questions people ask

What does Unable to play this content right now mean?
The app asked for the stream and did not get playable video back. It covers a dead stream, a refused connection and an expired subscription alike, because the server does not always say which.
Every channel stopped working at once.
Look at the account rather than the app. A reached connection limit and an expired subscription both do this, and both are visible under Account Information on an Xtream playlist.
I get sound but a black screen.
That is a video decoding problem, not a connection one — the stream is arriving. Switch Video decoder to Software in Settings, Playback, and try the channel again.
A channel disappeared from the list entirely.
Either its group is switched off under Settings, Playlists, Manage groups, or your provider removed it. Those are the only two possibilities — there is no per-channel hiding in this build.
It plays in another app but not in InoxTV.
Usually a codec the device's hardware decoder will not take. Check Audio track in the tool strip first, in case the channel carries a second one, then try the software audio decoder, and the software video decoder if the picture is the problem.
