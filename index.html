An IPTV player for Android phones, Android TV and Fire TV. Bring your own M3U, Xtream Codes or Stalker Portal playlist — InoxTV takes care of the guide, the recording and the remote.

InoxTV supports all three major IPTV playlist formats:

  • M3U / M3U8: Import via URL or local file. The most widely used format.
  • Xtream Codes: Connect with server URL, username, and password for categorized live TV, VOD, and series.
  • Stalker Portal: Connect using portal URL and MAC address with full STB emulation.

When that happens the fix is a better source, not a setting in the app: setting up the guide covers the order the pieces have to be in, and no guide data works through an empty one.

On Android TV, yes. Every key carries a short press and a long press, and both are reassignable in Settings → Remote control, which keeps three separate maps: Player, TV guide, and Movies and shows. Two actions in that list, Multiview and Picture-in-Picture, are not connected in this build, so a key assigned to either one does nothing.

⭐ Recommended

We recommend adding up to 5 playlists to InoxTV for the best experience. Here's why this is the ideal number:

  • Optimal Performance: Each playlist can contain thousands of channels, movies, and series. Keeping it to 5 ensures the app remains fast and responsive.
  • Faster EPG Updates: With fewer playlists, the Electronic Program Guide loads and refreshes much more quickly, so your TV guide is always up to date.
  • Smooth Navigation: Browsing between categories, searching across channels, and switching playlists stays buttery smooth with 5 or fewer playlists.
  • Lower Memory Usage: The app uses less device memory and storage, which is especially important on Android TV boxes with limited RAM.
  • Reliable Auto-Updates: Playlist auto-update scheduling works most reliably when managing up to 5 playlists, ensuring your content is always fresh.

💡 Tip: You can add playlists in any combination of formats — for example, 2 M3U playlists, 2 Xtream Codes, and 1 Stalker Portal. InoxTV reads all three side by side.

InoxTV includes a built-in backup and restore feature. Go to Settings → General, where Backup data and Restore data sit together, and write a backup file holding your playlists, favourites, key mappings and settings. Restoring it on another device rebuilds the same setup. What the file contains lists exactly what travels and what does not.

There is a four-digit PIN, and it is a configuration lock rather than a content filter. It closes the settings screen, or just Settings → Playlists and Settings → TV guide if you would rather leave the rest open, so a playlist cannot be swapped, your guide setup cannot be undone and the lock cannot be turned off without it. It does not restrict what plays: the Group options and Channel options entries in that list store your choice but nothing reads them in this build. The PIN is kept as a SHA-256 hash, so there is no recovery route. What the PIN locks spells out both halves.
