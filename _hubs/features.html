What each page here gives you

A feature page on this site is not a tour. Each one names the settings path the feature actually sits behind, lists every value its options accept and what choosing each one changes, and separates television behaviour from phone behaviour wherever the two differ. Labels are quoted the way the app spells them, punctuation included, so what you read matches the row you are looking at.

Where a control is on screen but not yet connected in this build, the page says so instead of describing what it looks like it ought to do. A settings row that quietly does nothing wastes more of your time than a missing one, so it is worth knowing which is which before you spend an evening testing it.

The two builds are not the same app

InoxTV ships as a television build and a mobile build. They share the playlist engine and the player, and they diverge wherever input matters: D‑pad focus, overlays, long presses, and menus that exist on one and not the other. A feature you reach from a remote menu on the television may live in a different place on a phone, which is why those differences get their own heading on a page rather than a parenthesis halfway down it.

If you are not sure which page you want

Start from what you are trying to do rather than from what the feature is called.

  • Watching and switching channels is live TV, and what is on later is the TV guide.
  • Films and series have their own library, described in movies and shows.
  • Getting back to something you have already watched splits between favourites and My list and search.
  • Picture problems that are settings rather than faults belong in playback and quality, and recording covers keeping a copy of what is playing.
  • Who can watch what is parental controls; how the app looks and what language it speaks is themes and languages.

If the behaviour you are seeing is a fault rather than a setting, troubleshooting will get you there faster than this section will.
